# Lab 00 — Ask the fleet

**Level:** L0 · observe

**What you're about to do:** ask an AI-generated wiki a few real questions
about a working system, then check its answers against the actual source.

**Why bother:** you're going to lean on AI answers about your own systems
sooner or later — a wiki, a chatbot, a copilot. Knowing how far to trust one,
and how to check it fast, is the whole skill. This is a safe place to practice
it, because nothing here is yours and nothing can break.

**Time:** about twenty minutes.

**You'll need:** a browser. No account, nothing to install.

## Steps

1. Pick one product repo: [solidago](https://github.com/lentago/solidago),
   [drosera](https://github.com/lentago/drosera),
   [kalmia](https://github.com/lentago/kalmia),
   [claytonia](https://github.com/lentago/claytonia), or
   [betula](https://github.com/lentago/betula).
2. Open its DeepWiki (the big badge near the top of the README) and ask the
   **Good first questions** the README carries. Then ask one question of your
   own — something you'd genuinely need to know to operate it.
3. Take one concrete claim from an answer and **verify it against the source**:
   find the file, workflow, or PR that proves or disproves it. The README's
   evidence links are your map.
4. Note one place the answer was vague, out of date, or wrong. (There usually
   is one — that's the point.)

## Proof

Comment on your lab-run issue with: the question you asked, the claim the
answer made, and a link to the source line that confirmed or refuted it —
plus your one "trust but verify" finding.

**How you know it worked:** you can point at one source line that backs an AI
claim, and one place the answer was vague, stale, or wrong. If you found both,
you're done.

**In your own org this looks like:** before you act on anything an AI tells you
about your systems — a generated runbook, a summary of last night's alerts, a
"here's what this config does" — you open the actual file and confirm it. The
answer gets you there faster; the source is still what you trust.
